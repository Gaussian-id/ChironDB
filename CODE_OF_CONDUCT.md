# ChironDB Code of Conduct

ChironDB welcomes people of all backgrounds and experience levels. Everyone
participating in issues, pull requests, discussions, or other project spaces
should be able to contribute without harassment or discrimination.

## How we work together

- Discuss ideas and evidence with respect. Critique the work without attacking
  the person who made it.
- Make room for different experiences, communication styles, and abilities.
- Give actionable feedback, accept corrections, and acknowledge mistakes.
- Respect other people's time, privacy, and boundaries.
- Help newcomers understand the project and its expectations.

Harassment, threats, discriminatory remarks, sexual advances, personal attacks,
and deliberate intimidation are unacceptable. So are publishing private
information without permission, repeated unwanted contact, and retaliation
against someone who raises a concern in good faith.

## Scope

This policy applies in ChironDB community spaces and when someone officially
represents the project elsewhere. Conduct outside these spaces may also be
considered when it directly threatens the safety of a project participant.

## Report a concern

Contact [Gaussian on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/gaussian-id/posts/)
by private message to arrange a confidential conversation with a maintainer.
Include relevant links or dates and any immediate safety or privacy concerns
once that channel is established. Do not post sensitive reports in public
comments, issues, or discussions. If a report concerns a maintainer, ask for
another Gaussian project owner to handle it.

Maintainers should acknowledge reports, keep information limited to the people
needed to handle the matter, and explain the outcome to those involved where
appropriate. Absolute confidentiality cannot be promised where there is a
legal obligation or an immediate safety concern.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove harmful content and restrict participation.
The response should reflect the seriousness, context, and impact of the
behaviour, as well as any repeated violations. Possible actions include:

1. A private correction explaining the expected change in behaviour.
2. A warning with clear boundaries and consequences for repetition.
3. A temporary restriction from project spaces or contact with participants.
4. A permanent ban for serious harm or repeated violations.

A serious incident may justify immediate restriction without an earlier
warning. Maintainers must not use moderation to silence respectful technical
disagreement. A person affected by a decision may ask a maintainer who was not
involved in the incident to review it, providing relevant context privately.
